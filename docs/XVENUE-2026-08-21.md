# Kalshi ↔ Polymarket cross-venue scanner — build note and first measurement

**Date:** 2026-08-21 · **Code:** `rhengine/xvenue.py` · **Tests:** `tests/test_xvenue.py` (19)
**Log:** `research_data/xvenue_scan.jsonl` · **Pairs:** `research_data/xvenue_pairs.json`

Read-only. Places no orders and imports nothing that can.

## The question

Does an executable price gap exist between Kalshi and Polymarket on the same
event, and is it worth taking? This note records the instrument and the first
two cycles. It is **not** a result — two snapshots on one afternoon measure
nothing about persistence, which is the only thing that matters.

## What the scanner prices in

The gap is not the edge. Three costs sit between them, and every logged row
carries all three so the log cannot flatter itself.

1. **Kalshi taker fee** `ceil(0.07 · C · P · (1−P))`, peaking at 1.75 ¢/contract
   at P = 0.50. The ceiling is per order, so `--contracts` matters: one contract
   at P = 0.5 pays 2 ¢ against a 1.75 ¢ theoretical fee. Maker fees exist on some
   series and are deliberately not modelled — an arb crosses.
2. **Capital locked to resolution, un-nettable across venues.** Every row carries
   `days_to_close` and `ann_return`. Read the annualized column first.
3. **Divergent resolution loses the stake, not the edge.** Cost + edge = 1 by
   construction, so the break-even probability of adverse divergence *equals the
   net edge in dollars*:

   > EV = (1−q)·edge − q·cost = 0 → q\* = edge/(edge+cost) = **edge**

   A 3 ¢ edge tolerates a 3 % chance the two venues resolve differently. Nothing
   more. `breakeven_divergence()` is that identity.

Leg risk cannot be measured from quotes and is not modelled.

## The identity turned out to be a mismatch detector

Two venues quoting the same event cannot sit 20 points apart with nobody closing
it. So a high q\* is not a large edge — it is a tell that the two legs are
**different contracts**. Rows above `MISMATCH_Q = 0.10` are flagged `MISMATCH?`
and excluded from the report's hit rate. This fell out of the arithmetic rather
than being designed in, and it earns its place: in the first live cycle every
one of the six largest "edges" was a mismatch and the flag caught all six.

**It is a coarse filter, not a substitute for reading the rules.** Two mismatches
it missed, both found by `--audit`:

- `KX2028DRUN-28-TWAL` (will Tim Walz **run**) paired against
  `will-tim-walz-win-the-2028-democratic-presidential-nomination` (will he
  **win**). Net +4.6 ¢, q\* 4.6 %, unflagged. Running and winning are different
  contracts.
- `KXVPRESNOMD-28-LJAM` (**Vice**-presidential nominee) paired against the
  **presidential** nomination question. Net +0.1 ¢, unflagged.

And a third that survives even a careful read. Kalshi `KXPRESNOMD-28-LJAM`
resolves YES only if the candidate *"wins **and accepts**"* the nomination;
Polymarket asks only whether they *"win"* it. A nominee who wins and declines
splits the pair and takes the whole stake. That single word is worth more than
every edge measured below.

## First measurement, 2026-08-21 17:28–17:29 UTC

Discovery: 8,256 open Kalshi markets × 500 Polymarket markets.

| stage | pairs |
|---|---|
| Jaccard ≥ 0.40 on title tokens | 3,140 |
| + subtitle gate (60 % of the Kalshi subtitle must appear on the Polymarket side) | **405** |

The subtitle gate is not optional. Without it every market on a multi-outcome
event shares the same title, the shared frame alone clears 0.40, and the matcher
pairs Gavin Newsom with Hillary Clinton — which it did. The gate cut 2,735
candidates and that was the dominant failure mode among them.

Scanning the 50 deepest by Polymarket liquidity, `--contracts 100`:

| group | net edge | days | annualized | q\* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `KX2028DRUN` / `KX2028RRUN` — **run** vs **win** | +4 ¢ … +24 ¢ | 498 | +3 % … +23 % | 4.6 % … 24 % |
| `KXPRESNOMD` / `KXPRESNOMR` / `KXPRESPERSON` — genuinely the same question | **+0.07 ¢ … +0.37 ¢** | 809–1174 | **+0.0 % … +0.1 %** | 0.1 % … 0.4 % |

**The finding, stated at its true width:** on the pairs that plausibly mean the
same thing, net edge after the Kalshi fee was under half a cent, on holds of two
to three years, annualizing to roughly zero. Every visibly large edge was a
contract mismatch. This is two cycles on one afternoon on one market family
(2028 US politics) — it establishes nothing about persistence, other families,
or fast-moving markets, which is what the log is for.

## Boundaries

- **Everything in the pairs file is `verified: false`.** The string matcher
  proposes; only a human reading both rules texts disposes. Every log row carries
  the flag so an unverified gap can never be read as a finding.
- **Polymarket rules text is not machine-readable.** Gamma exposes the question,
  not the UMA resolution criteria. `--audit` prints the Kalshi rules and the
  Polymarket URL; the second half is a human step and there is no way around it.
- **US legal access is unresolved here and is not a code question.** If a gap
  persists, the barrier producing it — legal, capital mobility, or both — must be
  named before anything is traded. A spread that survives is the price of a
  barrier, not free money.
- **Polymarket fees default to 0 bps** (`--poly-fee-bps`). True on most markets,
  not all. Verify per market before believing a row.
- **Sizes are top-of-book only.** No depth walk, so `max_contracts` overstates
  what fills at the quoted price.

## Not a trial

`trials.py` counts decision points resolved by looking at a number. Building an
instrument is not one. Reading this log and concluding "there is / is not an
exploitable gap" **is** one — log it to family `xvenue` at that point.

## Commands

```bash
python3 -m rhengine.xvenue --discover --limit 500 --min-overlap 0.40
python3 -m rhengine.xvenue --audit --top 20        # rules side by side
python3 -m rhengine.xvenue --once --top 50 --min-edge 0.01
python3 -m rhengine.xvenue --interval 300 --top 50 # poll every 5 min
python3 -m rhengine.xvenue --report                # persistence per pair
```

`hit_rate` in `--report` is the column to read. A gap in one cycle is a stale
quote; a gap in thirty-five of forty is a barrier.

(This repo's CLAUDE.md specifies `py` on the Windows box. The measurements above
were run with `python3` on darwin.)

# Part 2 — relations, and the arbs that do not need matching rules

**Added 2026-08-21 (same day).** Code: `rhengine/xvenue_relations.py`,
`tests/test_xvenue_relations.py` (23). Part 1's model was wrong in two ways and
both are corrected here.

## Wrong once: not every pair is EQUIVALENT

Part 1 priced every matched pair as a hold-to-resolution equivalence hedge. Most
pairs are not equivalent, and pricing them as if they were is what produced the
+24 ¢ "edges" on `KX2028DRUN` — Kalshi's *will X run* against Polymarket's
*will X win*. The gap between running and winning is not an edge. It is the
price of the difference.

## Wrong twice, and this is the useful half

Those pairs were thrown away as mismatches. They should not have been. When one
contract logically **implies** the other, the pair admits a hedge whose worst
case is bounded *by the logic* — which is a far weaker requirement than the two
venues resolving identically, and it is checkable.

Write B ⟹ A for "B is the narrower claim".

| relation | admissible hedge | payoff states | guaranteed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EQUIVALENT` | YES one, NO the other (either direction) | always 1 | **the rules matching** |
| `K_BROADER` (P ⟹ K) | YES Kalshi, NO Polymarket | 1 / **2** / 1 | logic |
| `K_NARROWER` (K ⟹ P) | NO Kalshi, YES Polymarket | 1 / **2** / 1 | logic |
| `DISJOINT` | NO both | 1 / 1 / **2** | logic |
| `UNKNOWN` | none | — | — |

Take `K_BROADER`. Buy YES on the broader leg and NO on the narrower:

- P = 1 (so K = 1): 1 + 0 = **1**
- P = 0, K = 1: 1 + 1 = **2**
- P = 0, K = 0: 0 + 1 = **1**

Minimum payoff 1, profit ≥ `p_bid − k_ask`. Nobody who wins a nomination on
Polymarket's reading failed to run for it on Kalshi's, so this tolerates the two
venues disagreeing about sources, cutoffs and timezones in a way an equivalence
never can. **The mirror direction pays zero in the middle state** — and it was
exactly the direction Part 1 was reporting as a 24 ¢ edge. The `HEDGES` table
offers one direction for a nested relation for that reason.

`q*` is now emitted **only** where `guarantee == "rules"`. A logic-guaranteed
hedge does not have the divergent-resolution failure mode; its residual risk is
that the *implication* breaks under one venue's rule set, which is a narrower
question and the one `--audit` should be asked.

## "The rules might not matter if the spread is wide enough" — right, and here it is

There is a second way out of resolution risk: **do not hold to resolution.**
`unwind()` prices it — enter the dislocation, exit on convergence, gone before
either venue resolves anything. It costs two Kalshi taker fees and both spreads
crossed twice, and `convergence_needed` is how far the two mids must close to
cover that.

It is a real answer and it is not free. The hold-to-resolution arb has a bounded
worst case; **this one has none**, and every row says so in `unwind_risk`. If the
gap is structural — running is not winning — it never converges and the exit is
at whatever the market gives.

## The classifier, and the two failures the audit caught

`classify()` parses each side into (claim, party, year, office, subject) and
compares on a lattice, `RUN > ADVANCE > NOMINEE > WIN_ELECTION`. It defaults to
`UNKNOWN` and that default is load-bearing.

Two refusals worth keeping:

- **Party must be fixed on both sides.** "Win the 2028 US Presidential Election"
  does not imply winning the *Republican* nomination — the winner could run as
  an independent. Downgraded to UNKNOWN, not quietly assumed.
- **Endorsement and ticket claims imply nothing** in either direction.

And two failures that only `--audit` found, after the first relation-aware scan
called both EQUIVALENT:

1. **`KXTRUMPPRES-28`** — "will a Trump **family member** be the 2028 Republican
   nominee" against "will **Eric Trump** win" it. A set against one of its
   members. That was the largest apparent edge in the run: **+5.6 ¢**. It is
   `K_BROADER`, and priced as such it disappears. Fixed by subject-scope
   detection.
2. **`KXSENATEPAD-28-JFET`** — Fetterman's **Senate** nomination against the
   **presidential** nomination. Both NOMINEE/DEM/2028, no shared outcome at all.
   Fixed by office comparison.

Same lesson twice, and a third time in `news.py`: **a shared frame produces
false signal, and you have to gate on the distinguishing term.**

## Second measurement, 2026-08-21 19:04 UTC

405 candidates classified: 149 UNKNOWN, 111 EQUIVALENT, 84 K_BROADER, 61 DISJOINT.
Scanning the 40 deepest of each class, `--contracts 100`, with Polymarket's real
NO-token book instead of the `1 − yes_bid` approximation:

| relation | best net edge | reading |
|---|---|---|
| `K_BROADER` | **−3.4 ¢** (all negative) | the bound p ≤ k holds everywhere. No violation, no trade — correctly. |
| `DISJOINT` | **−96 ¢** (all negative) | needs two *expensive* mutually exclusive legs; VP and P nominations of longshots are 1 ¢ each, so the bound is nowhere near violated. |
| `EQUIVALENT` | **+0.48 ¢** (`KXPRESNOMR-28-EM`) | q\* 0.5 %, 809-day hold, +0.2 %/yr. |

**Part 1's conclusion survives at higher resolution and is now better supported.**
The two largest apparent edges from Part 1 were pricing errors of my own making;
with the relations right, the best genuine dislocation in this corpus is half a
cent on a two-year hold. The DISJOINT class is real but structurally rare — it
needs two likely, mutually exclusive contracts, which longshot politics never
supplies.

Still two snapshots, still one market family, still every pair `verified: false`.

# Part 3 — the 15-minute BTC pair, gate written before the data

**Added 2026-10-09.** Code: `rhengine/xvenue_btc15.py`, `tests/test_xvenue_btc15.py`
(18). Read-only. Logs to `research_data/xvenue_btc15/` (gitignored, ~4 MB/day).

Kalshi `KXBTC15M` and Polymarket `btc-updown-15m-<start>` ask the same question on
the same quarter-hour grid, and both venues now charge a crypto taker fee of
`0.07 · P · (1−P)`. At 50¢ that is ~1.75¢ per leg, so **a pair needs the two asks to
sum to ≤ ~96.5¢ just to break even.**

Unlike Part 1, the hold is 15 minutes, so capital lockup is not the problem. The
problem is the **settlement source**: Kalshi uses CF Benchmarks' BRTI, Polymarket
names Chainlink's BTC/USD stream, and each sets its own starting line. When BTC
finishes between the two lines the venues disagree and the pair pays $0 (or $2).
Part 1's identity still holds: a net edge of X tolerates an X chance of adverse
disagreement. With 15-minute moves of ~±21 bps, a few bps of line difference is
several percent of windows. That is why this logger records **outcomes as well as
quotes**, and scores a paper trade on what actually settled.

**Gate, logged to family `xvenue` before any quote was collected:** at least 1,000
settled windows; at least 10% with net edge > 0 and at least $50 of depth on both
legs; median best edge above the measured disagreement rate; paper P&L > 0.

Boundaries: this reads Polymarket's international CLOB, not the Polymarket US app.
Top-of-book only. Both venue reads are sequential, and rows skewed > 2 s are
excluded.

```bash
python3 -m rhengine.xvenue_btc15 --once     # one snapshot, checks both venues answer
python3 -m rhengine.xvenue_btc15 --run      # log every 15 s, settle as it goes
python3 -m rhengine.xvenue_btc15 --report   # the gate
```

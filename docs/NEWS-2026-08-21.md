# News breadth against event-contract prices

**Date:** 2026-08-21 · **Code:** `rhengine/news.py` · **Tests:** `tests/test_news.py` (16)
**Log:** `research_data/news_scan.jsonl`

**This is the directional tool, not the arbitrage one.** `xvenue.py` finds trades
whose worst case is bounded by logic. Nothing here is bounded by anything. A
news-driven trade on an event contract is a bet that the market has not repriced
yet, and it can be wrong in the full amount. Read that again before sizing.

## Sources, and what each is actually for

| source | key | latency | covers | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Alpaca** `/v1beta1/news` | `ALPACA_API_KEY`, already set | seconds–minutes | equities, crypto; symbol-tagged | best latency here. Will not cover an Israeli election or a papal conclave |
| **Google News RSS** | none | minutes–hours | everything, incl. politics and geopolitics | the breadth source. One trap, below |
| **GDELT DOC 2.0** | none | 15 min | global, multi-language, tone + volume | **throttled to HTTP 429 from this host** even at one request per 25 s. Wired with backoff, returns `None` rather than raising |

Not wired: Finnhub and Alpha Vantage both have news endpoints and both already
have adapters here, but neither key is in `secrets.json` — only Alpaca and
Massive are (checked 2026-08-21). Add a key and they slot in beside Alpaca.

## The trap: Google News RSS is relevance-ranked, not recency-ranked

A bare query for `"Nikki Haley"` returns 100 items spanning **2024 to last week**.
Filtering that by timestamp afterwards does not window the sample, it throws most
of it away and leaves an arbitrary remnant. The window has to go **into the
query** as Google's `when:Nd` operator:

| query | items | newest |
|---|---|---|
| `"Nikki Haley"` | 100 | 8 days old |
| `"Nikki Haley" when:2d` | 12 | 3 hours old |

`google_news()` does this. `news_rate()` then runs **two** windowed queries — hot
and baseline — and compares rates, netting the hot window out of the baseline so
a spike cannot inflate its own denominator. The ~100-item cap is reported as
`saturated` rather than smoothed over: a saturated count is a floor, not a rate.

## The subject gate, and the false signal it killed

First live run, 8 NATO Secretary-General markets. `KXNEXTNATOSECGEN-99-ULEY`
(Ursula von der Leyen) came back **UNPRICED at ratio 6.0** — a six-fold coverage
spike with the contract dead flat. The top headline was *"Russian drones hit
border crossing with Moldova"*.

Two bugs, one lesson:

1. The name regex required `[A-Z][a-z]+` runs, so **"Ursula von der Leyen" parsed
   as "Ursula"** and the lowercase particles fell out. The query collapsed to
   generic NATO terms and retrieved the entire Ukraine news cycle.
2. Even with the name intact, a query built from the shared frame ("next NATO
   Secretary General") retrieves the frame's whole news cycle. Counting that as
   coverage *of one candidate* manufactures a spike from unrelated stories.

`on_subject()` is the fix: keep only headlines that actually contain the
distinguishing subject, and tell the caller via `subject_gated` when there was no
subject to gate on. After the fix that market reads QUIET with 0 on-subject
articles, which is correct.

This is the **third** time the same failure has appeared in one day — the pair
matcher's title frame, the relation classifier's set-versus-member, and now this.
A shared frame produces false signal. Gate on the distinguishing term.

## The four boxes

`classify_divergence()` joins the coverage rate to the contract's own price tape
(reusing `market_data_kalshi.fetch_prob_bars`, already cached):

| | price moved | price flat |
|---|---|---|
| **coverage spiked** | `PRICED` — the market saw it. No trade. | `UNPRICED` — the candidate |
| **no spike** | `SILENT_MOVE` — informed flow, or a thin book being pushed | `QUIET` |

`UNPRICED` is a **place to look, not a trade**. A flat price after real news
often means the news is not news to anyone who trades that contract. `SILENT_MOVE`
is the more dangerous box: fading it is a bet against someone who may know more
than you do.

## Boundaries

- No sentiment scoring. Free feeds disagree about it and a headline classifier is
  a research project. Article volume against its own baseline needs a timestamp
  and arithmetic, and that is all this claims to measure.
- Alpaca covers tickers, not events. Most Kalshi political and geopolitical
  contracts have no symbol, so they fall through to Google News.
- One RSS request pair per market. Keep watch lists short.
- Nothing here has been measured for predictive value. The boxes are a way to
  *look*, and the log exists so that a claim about them can eventually be tested.

## Not a trial, yet

Same rule as `xvenue.py`: building an instrument is not a decision resolved by
looking at a number. The first time this log is read and a claim made — "the
UNPRICED box predicts subsequent contract moves" — that **is** one. Log it to
family `news`, and note that the box definition itself has free parameters
(`spike_ratio`, `move_threshold`, hot and base windows) that must be counted.

## Commands

```bash
python3 -m rhengine.news --query '"Nikki Haley"' --hot 1d --base 7d
python3 -m rhengine.news --query 'NATO' --subject "von der Leyen"
python3 -m rhengine.news --alpaca --symbols AAPL,TSLA
python3 -m rhengine.news --series KXNEXTNATOSECGEN --limit 10
python3 -m rhengine.news --limit 15 --box UNPRICED
```

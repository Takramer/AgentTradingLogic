# Does anything predict the next hour of bitcoin?

**Date:** 2026-08-22 · **Code:** `rhengine/btc.py` · Trials: family `btc`, 14 logged
**Target:** `KXBTCD`, Kalshi's hourly BTC digital — 3.68M contracts/24h, 318 strikes,
settles on a 60-second BRTI average at the top of each hour.

## Method, and what makes it not a sweep

Eleven features, one horizon, one target, **written to the trial ledger before
the first IC was computed**. At eleven trials the family-wise bar is t = 2.83 and
luck alone scores E[max t] = 1.62. Three post-hoc looks (block stability,
volatility conditioning, and selecting a winner after seeing all eleven) were
logged afterwards, moving the family to 14 and the bar to **t = 2.91**.

Data: 13,490 Coinbase hourly bars, 2025-02-12 → 2026-08-22, two non-hourly gaps.
Validated against the settlement index itself — BRTI prints recovered from
settled Kalshi markets sit 2.0 bps from the Coinbase minute close at the
settlement instant (sd 4.1 bps) against a ~92 bps hourly move.

**The bar is execution, not significance.** An hourly digital trades near 50/50,
exactly where the Kalshi taker fee peaks at 1.75 ¢, and the tight strikes quote
1 ¢ wide. Fee plus half-spread puts break-even at a **52.26% hit rate**. Every
edge below is quoted against that, not against 50%.

Five clear t = 2.91, all with **negative** IC, all measuring "price is high
relative to recent". That is one finding — short-horizon mean reversion — not
five, and the five are heavily correlated with each other.

## The winner survives every check applied

`range_pos_24h` is where the close sits inside the trailing 24-hour high–low range.

**Chronological 60/40 split**, sign fixed on train and scored on test: sign held,
out-of-sample hit 0.5390, edge +1.64.

**Six equal chronological blocks — 6/6 positive after execution costs:**

| block | from | IC | hit | edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2025-02-12 | −0.0481 | 0.5338 | +1.12 |
| 2 | 2025-05-16 | −0.0520 | 0.5507 | +2.81 |
| 3 | 2025-08-16 | −0.0476 | 0.5518 | +2.92 |
| 4 | 2025-11-17 | −0.0417 | 0.5304 | +0.78 |
| 5 | 2026-02-18 | −0.0271 | 0.5360 | +1.34 |
| 6 | 2026-05-21 | −0.0232 | 0.5394 | +1.68 |

**Three volatility buckets — positive in all three:** +2.53 low, +0.84 mid,
+1.34 high. Not a regime artifact.

For contrast, `ret_4h` had the better out-of-sample t (−3.14) and fails both
checks: 3/6 blocks positive, and its edge is **negative in the high-vol bucket**.
The best out-of-sample t-stat was not the most robust feature, which is the whole
reason the block and regime checks exist.

## The boundary, stated with the claim

- **The IC is decaying.** Blocks 1–3 average −0.048; blocks 5–6 average −0.025.
  Halved over eighteen months. Extrapolated, this is gone within a year or two.
- **The edge is thin.** ~1.6 points over a 52.26% break-even, at 24 settlements a
  day. It survives the fee and half the spread and nothing else — no slippage, no
  partial fills, no adverse selection from resting orders.
- **It is one finding, not five.** The correlated features do not confirm each
  other.
- **Mean reversion at this horizon is liquidity provision.** Buying because price
  just fell means taking the other side of whoever needed to sell. That is a real
  business, but it is paid for bearing inventory risk, not for being clever.

## The test that has not been run, and why it decides everything

Everything above predicts **the sign of the underlying's next hourly return**. It
does not predict **whether the ladder is mispriced**. Those are different claims,
and the weather book died precisely on that distinction — three sessions were
spent calibrating a forecast before anyone asked how good the market's own
forecast was.

The same instrument applies here and the data is free: settled `KXBTCD` markets
carry `expiration_value`, and hourly candlesticks reconstruct any past ladder.
The required test is whether the signal beats the **ladder's own implied
probability**, hour by hour, not whether it beats a coin.

**Until that is run, nothing here is a trade.** A 54% hit rate against a coin is
worth nothing if the ladder was already quoting 55%.

# Part 2 — scored against the ladder  ⟨RETRACTED AND REDONE⟩

**Original run 2026-08-22, retracted the same day.** The first version of
`ladder_test()` reported a +0.284 partial correlation (t +3.29), a day-clustered
t of +4.20 positive on all five days, and a P&L backtest showing **47–62% ROI
over six days**.

**All of it was lookahead.** Coinbase stamps a candle with its bucket START, so
`closes[T]` is the price at **T+1h**. Reading `closes[settle-3600]` as "spot an
hour before settlement" actually reads the settlement price — measured at
**−0.4 bps, sd 2.7** against the BRTI print. The strike was chosen knowing the
answer, and the feature was computed through it.

The tell was there and was nearly missed: a 47% six-day ROI is not a finding, and
the market's Brier score came out **worse than always guessing 0.5**, which no
functioning market does. Both were dismissed as "the market is badly calibrated"
before the alignment was checked.

Corrected: the decision key is `settle − 2×3600`, because the market for
settlement S opens near S−59min so the information must be as of S−1h.
`tests/test_btc.py::TestCandleAlignment` pins it.

## Corrected result, 134 settled hours, 2026-08-17 → 2026-08-22

| | before (contaminated) | **after** |
|---|---|---|
| market Brier | 0.263 *(worse than a coin)* | **0.209** |
| market hit rate | 0.635 | **0.649** |
| signal vs outcome | IC +0.083, t +0.92 | IC +0.027, t **+0.30** |
| signal vs residual | IC +0.277, t +3.20 | IC +0.020, t **+0.23** |
| strike offset vs residual | −0.644 | +0.064 |
| **partial, offset removed** | +0.284, t +3.29 | **+0.033, t +0.37** |

The market now scores better than a coin, as it must. The mechanical
strike-offset confound vanished, because the strike is finally chosen at the
decision-time spot. And the signal adds **nothing**: t = 0.37 against a
family-wise bar of 3.06.

**Bitfinex is the history source, not the settlement source.** It sits +8.4 bps

near 16 bps against a ~92 bps move, which attenuates any IC measured on it.
Coinbase stays the source for anything touching a settlement.

Part 1's "halved in eighteen months" was sampling noise inside that band. The
recent two years are on the weak side of the range, not dying.

## What this does and does not settle

**Settles:** short-horizon mean reversion in hourly bitcoin is real, robust, and
old. Two independent venues, 115,000 bars, twelve years, every regime.

**Does not settle anything about trading it.** The Kalshi hourly BTC ladder has
existed for about a year. An effect can be entirely real in the underlying and
entirely priced in the contract, and Part 2 — the only test that touches the
ladder — found the residual relationship running the *opposite* way on the six
days of settled history that exist.

Eleven years of underlying data cannot fix a six-day sample of market quotes.
The bottleneck is unchanged and no price API addresses it: **`--ladder` weekly,
or Kalshi's history is gone.**

# Part 4 — the P&L, and the answer

**2026-08-22.** `rhengine/btc.py --backtest`. Trials 23, bar **t = 3.06**.

Real dollars on the banked quotes: enter by crossing (buy YES at the ask, buy NO
at 1−bid), pay the Kalshi taker fee, hold to settlement. 134 hours,
three pre-registered edge thresholds.

| mapping | threshold | trades | hit | P&L/contract | ROI | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean-reversion | 0.05 | 128 | 0.406 | **−0.069** | **−14.5%** | −1.73 |
| mean-reversion | 0.10 | 118 | 0.390 | −0.072 | −15.6% | −1.74 |
| mean-reversion | 0.15 | 110 | 0.400 | −0.058 | −12.6% | −1.34 |
| momentum (flip) | 0.05 | 121 | 0.479 | +0.017 | +3.6% | +0.39 |
| momentum (flip) | 0.10 | 113 | 0.487 | +0.019 | +4.1% | +0.44 |
| momentum (flip) | 0.15 | 99 | 0.475 | +0.021 | +4.7% | +0.47 |

**The mean-reversion signal loses money on the ladder at every threshold.** The
flip is +4% at t ≈ 0.44, indistinguishable from zero against a bar of 3.06.

## The conclusion, stated plainly

Short-horizon mean reversion in hourly bitcoin is **real, robust and old** —
12/12 years of negative IC across two venues and 115,000 bars (Part 3). The
Kalshi hourly ladder **already prices it**. Trading it means paying the fee and
the spread for information the quote already contains, which is exactly what the
−14% ROI is.

This is the fourth book measured and closed on the same principle, and the
second time in this project the lesson had to be learned the hard way: *a signal
that predicts the world is not a signal that beats the price.*

## What would change the answer

Nothing in the price data — Part 3 proved eleven years cannot help. It would take
either a signal the ladder demonstrably lacks, or a structural edge (maker
rebates, a venue with worse pricing). Both are different projects.

`--ladder` still banks hours and Kalshi still drops anything past six days. Worth
running weekly only if someone intends to test a *different* signal; there is no
point accumulating history for this one.

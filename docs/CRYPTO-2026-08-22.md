# Kalshi's BTC complex — one underlying, seven settlement horizons

**Date:** 2026-08-22 · **Code:** `rhengine/crypto.py` · **Tests:** `tests/test_crypto.py` (18)

## Why crypto is a different problem from weather

A temperature contract has an external fair value: a forecast estimates the
number it settles on. Bitcoin has none — nobody forecasts tomorrow's BTC price.

What it has instead is **one underlying settled at many horizons**, which can be
checked against *itself* with no external data:

| series | horizon | 24h volume | shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| `KXBTCD` | hourly | **3.68M** | digital, 318 strikes |
| `KXBTC15M` | 15 min | 868k | up/down vs the price 15 min prior |
| `KXBTCMAXMON` | monthly | 623k | **one-touch** — "ever above B" |
| `KXETHD` | hourly | 296k | digital, 390 strikes |
| `KXBTC` | hourly | 103k | range/bucket |
| `KXBTCMINMON` | monthly | 76k | one-touch down |

~5.3M contracts/24h, against ~1.6M for the whole temperature complex.

## Three checks, in order of what they assume

### 1. Monotonicity — zero assumptions, zero resolution risk

"BTC > 80k" implies "BTC > 79k", so the YES price must be non-increasing in
strike. Where `bid(K_hi) > ask(K_lo)`, buy YES at `K_lo` and NO at `K_hi`:

| state | payoff |
|---|---|
| S > K_hi | 1 + 0 = **1** |
| K_lo < S < K_hi | 1 + 1 = **2** |
| S < K_lo | 0 + 1 = **1** |

Structurally the same nested hedge as `xvenue_relations.K_BROADER`, but *better*:
one venue, one contract family, **one settlement rule**, so the cross-venue
divergence risk that killed the Kalshi↔Polymarket work does not exist here.

**Result: zero arbs across 708 strikes** (KXBTCD, KXETHD, KXBTC), on executable
quotes net of fee. The ladder is internally coherent.

One trap worth keeping: near the money the ladder shows things like `K=75,300
mid 0.615 spread 0.770` and non-monotone mids. Those are **one-sided books**, not
dislocations — `mid` on a 0.23/1.00 quote is fiction. Filtering to a real
two-sided spread is mandatory before fitting anything.

### 2. Implied vol term structure

One lognormal sigma fitted per expiry, executable strikes only, rmse 0.015–0.024:

| expiry | hours | strikes | implied ann | implied move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-22 07:00 | 0.9 | 21 | 61.0% | $486 |
| 2026-08-22 21:00 | 14.9 | 27 | 47.8% | $1,527 |
| 2026-08-28 21:00 | 158.9 | 41 | 52.3% | $5,449 |

### 3. One-touch barriers against the vanilla ladder's own vol

Reflection principle: for a driftless walk, P(touch) ≈ 2 × P(finish above).

**Measured ratio across every live barrier: 2.03–2.06.** The monthly barrier
ladder is consistent with the hourly vanilla ladder. Far barriers trade slightly
above a single-sigma model, which is what a volatility smile looks like — not a
mispricing. **No systematic edge found.**

## Realized vol, and a correction

3,594 hourly Coinbase bars, 2026-03-25 → 2026-08-22:

| window | realized ann |
|---|---|
| full sample | **39.6%** |
| last 30d | 35.8% |
| last 14d | 43.2% |
| last 7d | 57.1% |
| last 36h | 87.3% |

Flat at 37–40% whether sampled at 1h, 2h, 4h, 6h, 12h or 24h, so it is volatility
and not microstructure noise.

**An earlier pass in this session estimated realized vol at 86% from 36 hours of
BRTI prints and concluded implied was cheap. That was backwards** — 36 hours is
not a vol estimate. Implied of 48–61% sits *above* 30-day realized of 35.8%: an
ordinary variance risk premium, not an edge. Before acting on it, see
`docs/OPTIONS-SOURCES-2026-08-01.md` §6 — this repo already measured
premium-selling against the published CBOE record and found BXM's alpha is
**t = +0.62** against total return.

## The settlement index is free, from Kalshi

Contracts settle on CF Benchmarks' BRTI. Their API does not serve it publicly
(404 on `/index/BRTI`, 400 on `/values?id=BRTI`). It does not matter:
`brti_history()` reads `expiration_value` off settled hourly markets and recovers
the actual settlement prints, exactly one hour apart.

That gives a free calibration target. **Coinbase's 1-minute close at the
settlement instant is −2.0 bps from BRTI (sd 4.1 bps)** against a ~92 bps hourly
move — so Coinbase is a sound proxy with unlimited history.

**Alignment matters more than the proxy.** BRTI is a 60-second average *ending*
at the hour. Comparing it to the hourly candle close instead of the minute bar at
that instant gives a basis sd of **94 bps** and looks like a broken proxy. It is
a timestamp bug, not a data problem.

## The 15-minute up/down markets are unplayable

They sit at ~50/50, which is exactly where the Kalshi fee peaks:

| price | fee/contract | % of stake | break-even hit rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.50 | 1.75¢ | 3.5% | **51.75%** |
| 0.40 | 1.69¢ | 4.2% | 41.69% |
| 0.30 | 1.47¢ | 4.9% | 31.47% |

You need a **1.75 percentage point** directional edge on a fifteen-minute bitcoin
move. Over 15 minutes at 40% annualized vol the underlying moves about 0.21% with
no drift, and there are 96 settlements a day to pay that edge on. This is a house
game.

## Two bugs worth recording

1. **Root-finding on a signed error sum.** Digitals above spot fall as vol rises;
   digitals below spot rise. The sum is not monotone in sigma, bisection has no
   valid bracket, and the fit railed to its bound reporting **500% implied vol**.
   Fixed by grid-then-refine on squared error.
2. **A sign flip in the one-touch.** `N(-d2)` where the formula wants `N(d2)`
   returned 0.967 for a barrier spot cannot reach, manufacturing a **+76¢ edge**
   on $130,000 BTC. The regression test pins it at < 1e-6.

Both were caught because the outputs were checked against something — the
reflection ratio and a reachability sanity check — rather than believed.
